# Merchant Admin Guide

This guide walks a merchant through running a store on **commerce.hanzo.ai** — the
Hanzo Commerce admin console. Everything here happens in the browser dashboard; the
console talks to the commerce backend over the platform `/v1` API and scopes every
action to your **active organization** (your store's tenant).

Sign in with your Hanzo identity (native IAM PKCE via `hanzo.id`). The **org
switcher** in the top bar is your tenant selector — every product, order, and
setting you see belongs to the selected organization.

## 1. Onboard — create a store

1. Sign in and pick (or create) your organization from the org switcher.
2. Follow the onboarding flow to name your store, set its primary domain(s), and
   choose currency and region defaults.
3. The **dashboard checklist** tracks setup completion. It derives progress from
   live data — store details, first product, an enabled integration, a published
   listing — so it advances as you actually configure the store, not from a
   checkbox you tick.

Each store is one tenant: its own organization, its own scoped data. Invited
members share the store's entitlement (see [Team](#8-team)).

> **Store access is $20/month** on the `pro` plan. Every store carries one
> store-bound subscription and an idempotent 7-day trial. See
> [Billing](#6-billing-self-service).

## 2. Products (+ AI catalog)

Products live on the bare `/v1/product` resource.

- **Add a product** — open **Products → New**, fill in name, description, price,
  media, and options/variants (e.g. size, color), then save. Prices are stored in
  the smallest currency unit.
- **Variants** — define product options and the matrix of variants (SKU, price,
  inventory) for each combination.
- **Collections** — group products into merchandising collections for your
  storefront (see [Collections](#4-collections--inventory)).
- **AI catalog builder** — the AI dock can create products for you. The
  `create_product` command and the manual Products form call the **same** create
  path, so an AI-generated product is a first-class product you can immediately
  edit. Describe what you sell and let the assistant draft the catalog, then
  review and publish.

## 3. Orders & customers

- **Orders** — view, search, and manage orders as they come in. Open an order to
  see its line items, totals, payment status, and customer. Fulfillment actions
  (ship, cancel) and exchanges (return + replacement) are available per order.
- **Customers** — every buyer is a customer record with their order history and
  contact details.
- **Customer groups** — segment customers into groups (e.g. wholesale, VIP) for
  targeted pricing, promotions, or sales-channel access.

## 4. Collections & inventory

- **Collections** — curate products into named collections that your storefront
  renders as browsable groups.
- **Inventory** — track stock levels per variant. Inventory is authoritative on
  the backend; the storefront and checkout read the current level so you don't
  oversell.

## 5. Tax, regions & sales channels

- **Regions** — a region bundles a set of countries with a currency and its tax
  behavior. Create a region per market you sell into.
- **Tax** — configure tax regions and tax rates; the platform calculates tax at
  checkout from the buyer's region. You can define rate rules and, where enabled,
  a tax provider.
- **Sales channels** — a sales channel is a storefront surface (web store, a
  marketplace, a point of sale). Assign products and publishable API keys to a
  channel so each surface sees only its own catalog.

## 6. Billing (self-service)

Manage your store's own subscription and wallet from **Billing**. All of these are
your-org-scoped and self-service:

- **Subscribe / change plan** — start or change your store subscription. Store
  access is the `$20/month` `pro` plan; an idempotent **7-day trial** is available
  once per store.
- **Payment methods** — add, update, or remove a card on file. Payment provider
  credentials never touch the browser — they are held in KMS.
- **Invoices** — view and download your billing history.
- **Top-up** — add prepaid balance to your wallet. Your balance is split honestly
  into **credits** (granted, non-cash) and **prepaid** (real money you topped up);
  the dashboard shows the two distinctly. Non-metered usage draws credits first,
  then prepaid; metered/GPU usage draws prepaid only and requires a card on file.

> Billing is strictly per-org: you can only ever view and change **your own**
> store's billing. (A platform admin can retarget a billing view across orgs; a
> merchant never can.)

## 7. Integrations

Open **Integrations** to connect payment and service providers.

- Providers read and toggle through `/v1/c/:org/integrations`.
- **Secrets never enter the browser** or your organization's data rows. A provider
  becomes toggleable only after its credentials exist in KMS.
- **Square** is built in and resolves its public browser configuration through the
  billing payment-config endpoint. Stripe connects via OAuth.

## 8. Team

Invite teammates to help run the store. Invited members share the store's
subscription entitlement — they don't each need their own store subscription.
Roles determine what a member can do; an **org admin** can manage store settings,
while ordinary members are scoped to their granted permissions.

## 9. API keys

Issue keys for programmatic and storefront access under **API keys**:

- **Publishable keys** — safe for the browser/storefront; scoped to a sales
  channel, they authorize the public storefront read + the public form-create
  path. They cannot perform admin actions.
- **Storefront token** — mint a per-org **Published** storefront token
  (`POST /v1/store/storefront-token`) for your storefront's server-side BFF to
  drive checkout. The storefront BFF (holding this token or the service token) is
  the one legitimate checkout entry point — a browser must never mint checkout
  directly with a client-chosen org.

Keep secret keys server-side only; publishable keys are the sole browser-safe
credential.
