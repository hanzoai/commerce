# Retention of Records

## Scope
All of [ORG-NAME]'s records, be they analog or digital, are subject to the
requirements of this procedure.

## Responsibilities
1. Asset owners are responsible for ensuring that all personal data is collected, retained and destroyed in line with the requirements of the GDPR.
2. The CFO is responsible for financial (accounting, tax) and related records.
3. The head of HR is responsible for the retention of all HR records.
4. The Health and Safety Officer is responsible for retention of all Health and Safety records.
5. The Company Secretary is responsible for retention of all other statutory and regulatory records.
6. The Data Protection Officer is responsible for storage of data in line with this procedure.
7. The Continuity Officer is responsible for ensuring that retained records are included in business continuity and disaster recovery plans.
8. One natural person may fill multiple roles, and any roles
   not filled by a duly acknowledged executive (as per [ORG-NAME]'s bylaws) is
   considered an employee, with all the rights and responsibilities accorded to
   them by the state of [ORG-STATE-FORMATION].

## Procedure
1. Financial records.
..1. Financial records are retained no less than 3 years, beginning in the fiscal year in
which the record was created. Records may be retained longer at the discretion
of the responsible party, in order to comply with local, state, or federal tax
laws, insurance requirements, creditor requirements, etc.
..2. Records may be stored either electronically or in analog accounting
systems.
..3. Analog records are to be disposed of by shredding. Digital records are to
be disposed of by irrevocable deletion. Physical media containing these records
is to be irrevocably erased, via magnetic erasure or physical destruction.
..4. Cryptographic keys used to encrypt records are _not_ considered part of the
records themselves and may be retained.
..5. Third parties may be used to retain this data, if and only if their
Retention of Records Procedure is in accordance with [ORG-NAME]'s. Third parties
retaining digital financial data must be able to provide irrevocable deletion.

2. HR records.
..1. HR records are retained no less than 6 years, beginning in the fiscal year in
which the record was created. Records may be retained longer at the discretion
of the responsible party, in order to comply with local, state, or federal
employment laws, insurance requirements, creditor requirements, etc.
..2. Records may be stored either electronically or in analog workforce
systems.
..3. Analog records are to be disposed of by shredding. Digital records are to
be disposed of by irrevocable deletion. Physical media containing these records
is to be irrevocably erased, via magnetic erasure or physical destruction.
..4. Cryptographic keys used to encrypt records are _not_ considered part of the
records themselves and may be retained.
..5. Third parties may be used to retain this data, if and only if their
Retention of Records Procedure is in accordance with [ORG-NAME]'s. Third parties
retaining digital labor data must be able to provide irrevocable deletion.

3. Health and Safety records.
..1. Health and Safety records are retained no less than 3 years, beginning in the fiscal year in
which the record was created. Records may be retained longer at the discretion
of the responsible party, in order to comply with local, state, or federal
employment laws, insurance requirements, creditor requirements, etc.
..2. Records may be stored either electronically or in analog workforce
systems.
..3. Analog records are to be disposed of by shredding. Digital records are to
be disposed of by irrevocable deletion. Physical media containing these records
is to be irrevocably erased, via magnetic erasure or physical destruction.
..4. Cryptographic keys used to encrypt records are _not_ considered part of the
records themselves and may be retained.
..5. Third parties may be used to retain this data, if and only if their
Retention of Records Procedure is in accordance with [ORG-NAME]'s. Third parties
retaining digital safety data must be able to provide irrevocable deletion.

4. Other statuory records.
..1. Statuory records not covered above will be handled by the abovementioned
responsible party as they are identified. Precise retention requirements will be
added to this documention as they are refined.
..2. In the meantime, statuory records identified as important
may be stored either electronically or in analog systems.
..3. Analog records are to be disposed of by shredding. Digital records are to
be disposed of by irrevocable deletion. Physical media containing these records
is to be irrevocably erased, via magnetic erasure or physical destruction.
..4. Cryptographic keys used to encrypt records are _not_ considered part of the
records themselves and may be retained.
..5. Third parties may be used to retain this data, if and only if their
Retention of Records Procedure is in accordance with [ORG-NAME]'s. Third parties
retaining digital data must be able to provide irrevocable deletion.

5. Access to records
[ORG-NAME] acknowledges that certain User Groups require access to the
abovementioned records to complete their daily tasks. These User Groups each
have defined protocols for accessing this data that are described in the Rights
for User Groups document.
